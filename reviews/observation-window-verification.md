# Independent verification of shortened playback observation

**Pass.** In the field study's Methods and Table 1, page 5 specifies one minute of observation from playback onset. It defines response latency from playback onset to the first response by a member of the group. I independently parsed the raw playback CSV with explicit pairing by experiment ID and stimulus, requiring a shared date for each pair. Of 139 pairs on 12 dates, 49 match in both recorded distance and group size, spanning the same 12 dates. All 172 flagged behavior latencies are finite and within 0–47.8 seconds. The earliest flagged onset is zero in eight natural and one control trial. One natural trial has earliest onset exactly at 5 seconds and counts at the inclusive 5-second cutoff. The 60-second counts reproduce the overall response flags in all 278 trials. [Field protocol](https://doi.org/10.12688/openreseurope.24618.1) · [Frozen specification](../docs/model-spec.md#q3q4-shortened-observation-freeze)

For each pair I subtracted the control event indicator from the natural indicator at each inclusive cutoff, subtracted the 60-second contrast from each shorter-window contrast, and computed the difference in waiting times restricted to 60 seconds. For each sample I restarted seed 20261208, drew 10,000 date-block samples, concatenated actual pair indices with replacement and computed percentile intervals. I also removed each date in turn. All eight summary rows and all 96 date-deletion rows matched the production tables within absolute tolerance 10^-12. In matched pairs, reducing the window to 5 seconds missed ten natural events and one control event, lowering the paired risk difference by 18.37 percentage points (95% interval −34.69 to −4.76). The change stayed negative on removal of any single date (−21.95 to −9.76 points). The 15-second window missed one natural event. Every matched 60-second event occurred by 30 seconds. The sensitivity sample contained a control event at 47.8 seconds that the 30-second window would miss. These comparisons describe truncation of recorded events under the field protocol.
