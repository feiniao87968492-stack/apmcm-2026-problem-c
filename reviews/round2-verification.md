Reconstructing date-by-location identifiers from Data01 reproduced all 16 groups, all 108 labels, and five disjoint folds with at least four recordings per class in each test fold. Independent fits matched all 324 held-out predictions, including the logistic and forest predictions already present in the round-one table. Confusion matrices gave macro F1 of 0.314408, 0.369801 and 0.383395 for logistic, forest and boosting. Two-voice recall was 2/24, 1/24 and 4/24. The paired group bootstrap gave a boosting macro F1 gain of 0.068987 against logistic (95% interval −0.017090 to 0.125600), and 0.013594 against forest (−0.068930 to 0.098024). The two-voice point recall improved relative to both controls, while the F1 intervals include zero. The frozen rule retains logistic. ## Q2. Component omission and propagation ranks

I computed available-case means from the original representation files, used an independent adjacent-block pooling procedure for the nonincreasing curves, and solved each crossing on the log-distance scale. Matched row resamples reproduced all 48 radii, their marginal and paired percentile intervals, bounded support counts and point ranks. At 0.40, 0.50 and 0.60 all four definitions rank two-voice, modulated and flat from longest to shortest. At 0.70, omitting either spectrogram or amplitude spectrum gives modulated, flat and two-voice in that order. The two-voice radius moves from 79.27 m to 12.19 m without spectrogram and 12.03 m without amplitude spectrum. Omitting the envelope preserves the point order and extends the two-voice radius to 140.67 m. The results support keeping the full equal-weight index and reporting omission sensitivity. The bootstrap intervals condition on treating wide rows as the sampling unit. [Radius comparisons](../results/round2_q2_radii.csv)

I reconstructed the masks and five expID folds from Data02, then refit each model with fold-local standardization. The original empirical stimulus baseline and full multinomial fold scores reproduce. All nested fold scores and paired intervals match the published tables. Adding the call block increases both point losses. None of the blocks meet the joint criterion. These contrasts describe contributions along the frozen model sequence. ## Q4. Propagation fidelity and distance-adjusted behavior

I reconstructed the fidelity predictor at each exact retained distance from Data03–Data11, then independently refit both binary models. The original folds retained 252 rows from 135 experiments, with 117 complete pairs and 18 rows whose paired stimulus fell outside 20–150 m. Keeping each experiment within one fold and weighting by retained row count within fold reproduced coverage and the bootstrap intervals. Adding fidelity changes binary log loss from 0.490586 to 0.495399, a difference of +0.004813 (95% interval −0.002691 to +0.012438). Brier changes from 0.155666 to 0.156630, a difference of +0.000965 (−0.000930 to +0.002814). The frozen joint criterion rejects adopting the addition. [Coverage](../results/round2_q4_coverage.csv) · [Fold scores](../results/round2_q4_fold_scores.csv) · [Comparisons](../results/round2_q4_comparison.csv)

Pooled Pearson correlation between fidelity and log distance was −0.657. A linear regression on log distance and assigned call gave R² = 0.843. Within-call correlations were −0.984, −0.845 and −0.759 for flat, modulated and two-voice. Although the propagation observations are independent of the playback rows, each evaluated fidelity is fixed by assigned call and distance. Including it in a logistic model enriches the distance and call terms and tests held-out prediction in that model class. Its effect cannot be separately identified at fixed call and distance in these data. The operational product of fidelity and distance-adjusted response can count the same distance-related degradation in both components. It remains interpretable as a scenario score.
