| Question | Fixed choice and assumption | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 features | Use Duration, F01, Fpeak, Q25, Q50 and Q75 for the primary classifier. Describe F02 and PulseRate where measured. Use F02 availability as a diagnostic and fold-median pulse imputation as a sensitivity. | F02 is present only for two-voice calls. Pulse rate is absent in all 44 flat calls and in 10 other recordings. Using universally observed features limits dependence on the recording schema. |
| Q1 inference | Use available observations in Kruskal–Wallis and Mann–Whitney tests, with separate Holm corrections for omnibus and pairwise families. Evaluate a standardized L2 multinomial classifier and fold-majority baseline using five stratified date-by-location grouped folds. | Q2 fit | Compute observed means per acoustic representation and distance, clip the weighted mean to [0,1], fit downward isotonic regression and interpolate in log distance over 4–256 m. Use equal weights as primary. Use (0.45,0.45,0.10), (0.25,0.25,0.50) and unconstrained interpolation as checks. | The log scale follows the doubled distance design. Average signal quality is expected to decline with distance, although individual readings fluctuate. Equal weighting avoids estimating perceptual importance from an unrelated behavioral experiment. |
| Q2 uncertainty | Use fixed thresholds 0.40, 0.50, 0.60 for potential, standard and reliable recognition and 0.70 as a stricter scenario. Independently resample complete wide rows by representation. Repeat with aligned-row draws when missingness masks match. At support limits report bounds. | Thresholds turn signal similarity into an operational range. Distance observations stay together within each sampled row. The attachments lack acquisition identifiers, so both interval conventions are conditional on their row-resampling assumptions. |
| Q3 behavior | Encode head, vocal and approach with bits 1, 2 and 4. Compare paired any-response rates descriptively. Fit a standardized L2 multinomial model (C=1) to mask using assigned call, stimulus, interaction, context, log(1+distance) and log(1+group size). Validate in five expID-grouped folds against pooled training frequencies and resample pairs intact. | The model accounts for co-occurring behaviors using one probability distribution. Ninety pairs differ in playback distance, so each row retains its recorded covariates. Adjusted stimulus contrasts use identical covariates. Regularization stabilizes sparse behavior combinations. |
| Q3 latency | For each behavior fit ridge regression (alpha=5) to log(1+seconds) among observed responders, with a Gaussian residual model on that scale. Report response probability, conditional median and sample support at 50, 100 and 150 m and eight birds. Average contexts using empirical frequencies and weight the conditional latency mixture by event probability. A stratum with no observed latency has no estimated median. Flag fewer than five observed latencies within 25 m as sparse. | The transformation keeps observed zero onsets. The two outcomes address different questions about behavior. The support flag gives the reader local sample context. |
| Q4 integration | Combine fidelity and standardized natural-response probability by multiplication. Use the common 20–150 m domain, a 0.5 m grid, median group size eight and empirical context weights. Use the final above-cutoff location to interpolate the sustained crossing at 50% of the call's clean index at 20 m. Keep that reference fixed across scenarios. Check 40% and 60%, component-only models and Q2 weight choices. | A call should retain signal and elicit behavior to score strongly. A relative clean reference measures retention within a call type, so absolute index values also accompany radius comparisons. Common support keeps comparisons within all three call domains. |
| Q4 scenarios | Gate the index below fidelity thresholds 0.50, 0.60 and 0.70. Evaluate uniform losses of 5%, 10%, 15% and 20%. Apply the resulting retention to the positive natural-response excess over its matched control prediction. For expected counts assume 25%, 50% or 75% of eight birds respond when any response occurs. Pair Q2 and Q3 draws by index from separately seeded analyses. | These transparent perturbations isolate how the operational result depends on recognition, retention and the group-to-individual assumption. The two independent sources of data give a conditional combined uncertainty assessment. |
